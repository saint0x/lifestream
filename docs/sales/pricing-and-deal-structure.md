# Pricing And Deal Structure

## Pricing Principle

Vanta should not price purely on raw views.

Vanta should price on:

- Qualified Viewers;
- Verified Viewer Score;
- Creator Attention Value;
- watch time;
- retention;
- creator-category fit;
- sponsor exclusivity;
- placement depth;
- creator promotion commitments;
- campaign duration;
- and strategic value of the advertiser.

The pricing argument is:

> Verified attention should command more than undifferentiated impressions.

Qualified Attention is a pricing input, not a magic price override.

Vanta should use it to justify premium value, but clearing prices must ultimately be calibrated against:

- what advertisers actually pay;
- what renews;
- what categories command scarcity;
- what creators reliably deliver;
- what business outcomes advertisers value;
- and how much service work each package requires.

## Pricing Floors

Pricing floors protect the sales organization from low-ticket operational drag.

Suggested starting floors:

| Package | Starting Range |
| :-- | --: |
| Self-Serve Starter Buy | $1,000-$5,000 |
| Qualified Attention Pilot | $5,000-$25,000 |
| Creator Launch Package | $10,000-$50,000 |
| Season Sponsorship | $25,000-$150,000 |
| Category Ownership | $75,000-$500,000+ |

These are starting ranges, not permanent law.

The clearing price should adjust based on measured demand, creator strength, audience quality, and renewal behavior.

## Deal Inputs

Every proposal should specify:

- creator or category being bought;
- series or programming context;
- campaign dates;
- placement type;
- expected creator promotion;
- expected Qualified Attention range when available;
- deliverables;
- brand safety exclusions;
- reporting package;
- makegood policy;
- price;
- payment terms;
- renewal option.
- primary success metric;
- secondary success metrics;
- attribution method if performance is part of the deal;
- third-party verification status when applicable.

## Creator Economics

Vanta should preserve creator alignment.

The creator should feel:

> If I make great exclusive content and promote it hard, I can earn more.

The default structure should be:

```text
Advertiser pays campaign budget
-> Creator receives majority share
-> Vanta retains platform / marketplace / sales fee
```

The exact split may vary by deal type, production support, sales burden, platform contribution, and negotiated creator agreement.

But the principle should remain clear:

> Vanta makes money when creators make money.

## Deal Types

### Fixed Sponsorship

Advertiser pays a fixed amount for defined placement.

Best for:

- early campaigns;
- season sponsorships;
- creator launch packages;
- brand campaigns.

Pros:

- simple;
- easy to sell;
- easy for creators to understand.

Risk:

- Vanta must define delivery expectations carefully.

### CPM Or Qualified-CPM

Advertiser pays based on delivered impressions or Qualified Viewers.

Best for:

- repeat buyers;
- scalable category inventory;
- campaigns with reliable delivery forecasts.

Pros:

- maps to familiar media-buying logic;
- can scale.

Risk:

- may drag Vanta toward commodity pricing if not framed around quality.

### Hybrid Sponsorship Plus Performance Bonus

Advertiser pays a base sponsorship plus upside for performance.

Best for:

- mid-market advertisers;
- brands with strong conversion events;
- campaigns where Vanta wants to reduce buyer risk without giving away premium inventory.

Pros:

- creates aligned upside;
- easier for performance buyers.

Risk:

- attribution debates can consume time if rules are unclear.

Rules:

- define the conversion event before launch;
- define the attribution window before launch;
- define which links, codes, pixels, or post-view metrics count;
- define whether creator promotion affects eligibility;
- define whether Vanta, advertiser, or a third party is the source of truth;
- do not accept performance-only economics for premium inventory without strategic reason.

### Category Exclusivity Premium

Advertiser pays extra to block direct competitors in a category for a defined campaign.

Best for:

- large brands;
- seasonal campaigns;
- category ownership packages.

Pros:

- increases deal size;
- makes sponsorship feel strategic.

Risk:

- limits available inventory for other buyers.

## Discounting Rules

Discounting should be rare and strategic.

Acceptable reasons:

- first case-study customer;
- multi-campaign commitment;
- strategic brand logo;
- creator relationship value;
- category proof;
- fast close with upfront payment.

Bad reasons:

- buyer asks casually;
- salesperson wants an easier close;
- low-budget buyer wants high-touch service;
- Vanta cannot explain its own value.

If Vanta discounts, it should get something back:

- case-study rights;
- testimonial;
- renewal commitment;
- upfront payment;
- multi-creator expansion;
- flexible scheduling;
- reduced service load.

## Makegood Rules

Do not promise perfect performance.

Promise transparent measurement and clear delivery rules.

Any makegood should be defined before launch:

- what metric triggers it;
- what inventory can be used;
- how long the makegood window lasts;
- whether creator promotion underdelivery affects eligibility;
- whether advertiser creative delays affect eligibility.

Never let vague disappointment become an unlimited future obligation.

## Outcome Claims

Do not promise business outcomes without a measurement structure.

Acceptable:

> This package is designed to deliver qualified reach and attention among [audience].

Acceptable:

> This hybrid package includes a performance component tied to [defined event] measured through [defined method].

Not acceptable:

> This will definitely convert.

Not acceptable:

> We guarantee brand lift without a brand-lift study.

Premium sellers can be aggressive without being sloppy.

## Minimum Viable Rate Card

The first rate card can be simple:

| Product | Buyer Lane | Price Logic | Service Level |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Self-Serve Starter Buy | Low-Touch | Fixed menu | Template only |
| Qualified Attention Pilot | Mid-Market | Fixed test | Limited customization |
| Creator Launch Package | Mid-Market / Brand | Fixed plus creator strength | Standard proposal |
| Season Sponsorship | Brand / Agency | Creator, season, exclusivity | High-touch |
| Category Ownership | Brand / Agency | Category scale, exclusivity, duration | High-touch |

This should evolve with actual campaign data.

The point is not to invent perfect pricing on day one.

The point is to prevent every deal from becoming custom.
