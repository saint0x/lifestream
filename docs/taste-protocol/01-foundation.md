# Foundation

## Purpose

This questionnaire is designed to extract the decision-making system of an elite executive director, filmmaker, editor, cinematographer, and creative producer into a structured archive that can later function as the taste layer for Vanta's automated media-production system.

The creator-facing purpose is improvement. The protocol should help creators understand why work feels strong or weak, what to fix first, when imperfection is useful, and how to make a Vanta series feel more intentional without requiring wasteful production spend.

The goal is not simply to document:

- camera settings
- editing tricks
- lighting techniques
- shot lists
- pacing rules
- production conventions

The real objective is to capture:

> How an elite creative director decides what should happen, when it should happen, why it should happen, and when the normal rule should be broken.

The eventual Vanta Taste Protocol should allow an intelligent system to take raw footage and reason through questions such as:

- What is the actual story hidden inside this footage?
- Which moments are meaningful?
- Which moments are visually weak but narratively necessary?
- Which moments deserve silence?
- Which cuts should be invisible?
- Which cuts should be aggressive?
- What should the audience feel at this exact moment?
- What shot should follow this shot?
- When should the camera feel controlled?
- When should it feel unstable?
- How long is too long?
- When does imperfection create authenticity?
- When does imperfection simply look amateur?
- What separates cinematic restraint from boring footage?
- What separates energetic editing from cheap editing?
- What visual choices make a piece feel expensive?
- What makes something feel artistically intentional rather than merely polished?

The questionnaire should be answered with specific rules, examples, thresholds, exceptions, and reasoning wherever possible.

## 1. Definition Of Quality

1. What does "high quality" mean to you in moving-image media?
2. What does "cinematic" actually mean?
3. What are the most common things people call cinematic that are not actually cinematic?
4. What makes a piece of media feel expensive even when it was inexpensive to produce?
5. What makes expensive production still look cheap?
6. What is the difference between polished, cinematic, beautiful, stylish, tasteful, compelling, and emotionally powerful?
7. Rank those qualities by importance.
8. Which qualities can compensate for weaknesses in others?
9. Which weaknesses cannot be compensated for?
10. What are the minimum standards below which footage should never ship?
11. What qualities make you immediately trust a filmmaker?
12. What qualities immediately signal amateur work?

## 2. Taste Hierarchy

For each conflict below, explain which side normally wins and under what circumstances the opposite should win.

1. Story vs. visual beauty.
2. Emotion vs. technical perfection.
3. Authenticity vs. polish.
4. Clarity vs. mystery.
5. Pace vs. atmosphere.
6. Continuity vs. emotional impact.
7. Realism vs. stylization.
8. Performance vs. composition.
9. Clean audio vs. authentic environmental sound.
10. Consistency vs. surprising visual choices.
11. Audience comprehension vs. artistic ambiguity.
12. Efficiency vs. indulgence.
13. Creator personality vs. directorial authorship.
14. Brand consistency vs. project-specific identity.
15. Trend relevance vs. timelessness.
16. What is the overall hierarchy used when several of these priorities conflict simultaneously?
17. Which principles are absolute?
18. Which principles are merely defaults?
