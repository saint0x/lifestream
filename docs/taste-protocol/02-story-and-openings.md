# Story And Openings

The creator benefit of better story judgment is immediate: stronger openings earn more trust, reduce drop-off, and make the creator's best material easier for audiences to feel.

## 3. Finding The Story

Assume you receive 30 minutes of completely unedited footage.

1. What do you look for first?
2. How do you identify the true subject of the footage?
3. How do you identify the emotional center?
4. How do you distinguish the apparent story from the actual story?
5. What makes a moment narratively significant?
6. What makes a moment disposable?
7. How do you detect setup, tension, escalation, discovery, reversal, payoff, and resolution?
8. What should happen when the footage contains no obvious narrative arc?
9. How aggressively should chronology be rearranged?
10. When should chronological order remain untouched?
11. When should an ending be shown near the beginning?
12. When should context intentionally be withheld?
13. How do you create narrative tension from ordinary events?
14. When is doing so manipulative or dishonest?
15. How much narrative restructuring is acceptable before the edit misrepresents reality?

## 4. The First 5-60 Seconds

1. What must happen in the first 2 seconds, 5 seconds, 15 seconds, 30 seconds, and 60 seconds?
2. What makes a strong cold open?
3. When should there be no cold open?
4. What makes a hook feel sophisticated rather than algorithmic?
5. What opening techniques feel cheap or overly social-media-native?
6. When should a piece begin mid-action, with silence, with dialogue, with an establishing shot, with a detail, with narration, or with a visual mystery?
7. How quickly should the audience understand what they are watching?
8. When is disorientation desirable?
9. What information should never be revealed too early?
10. What is the difference between intrigue and confusion?
